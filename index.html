RESEARCH AT THE INTERSECTION OF GEOSCIENCE & ENGINEERING

From clay minerals to sustainable subsurface systems.

I am an Assistant Professor at Nanyang Technological University. My research connects molecular-scale interactions, geomaterial microstructure, and coupled multiphysics to address challenges in environmental and energy geotechnics.

RESEARCH DIRECTIONS

Exploring connections
across scales.

From atomistic descriptions of clay–water interfaces to predictive models of complex underground environments.

01

Clay across scales

Molecular and coarse-grained simulations of clay hydration, aggregation, transport, and mechanical behavior.

02

Environmental barriers

Contaminant retention, PFAS, nanoplastics, and long-term performance of engineered clay systems.

03

Coupled multiphysics

Multiphase fluid flow, heat transport, mechanical deformation, and reactive transport in porous media.

04

Subsurface energy

CO₂ geological storage, geological hydrogen, geothermal systems, and underground infrastructure.

ABOUT

Research driven by
fundamental questions.

Before joining NTU, I was an Associate Research Scholar and Postdoctoral Research Associate at Princeton University. I received my Ph.D. in Petroleum and Geosystems Engineering from the University of Texas at Austin.

My group combines computational methods and experiments to understand how microscopic processes govern the macroscopic behavior of geomaterials.

## Engineered clay barriers

Bentonite clay is used as an engineered barrier for isolating high-level radioactive waste and experiences strong thermo-hydro-mechanical-chemical (THMC) gradients. We use large-scale molecular dynamics (MD) to predict properties of compacted bentonite and to inform long-term assessments.

Relevant publications:
- Zheng & Bourg (2023), *ACS Nano*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsnano.3c05751)
- Zheng, Underwood & Bourg (2023), *Applied Clay Science*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clay.2023.106964)

## Mesoscale geomaterials

Our coarse-grained models simulate thousands of smectite platelets to study hierarchical aggregation, consolidation, microstructure, dynamics, and coupled material properties at larger scales.

Relevant publications:
- Shen, Zheng & Bourg (2025), *Journal of Colloid and Interface Science*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcis.2024.12.053)
- Zheng, Shen & Bourg (2025), *Journal of Colloid and Interface Science*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcis.2025.137573)
- Zheng & Bourg (2026), *Journal of Physical Chemistry C*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpcc.6c00004)

## Geopolymer engineering

We investigate mineral–binder interfaces in fly-ash-stabilized marine clay and alkali-activated aluminosilicate materials. Molecular calculations are used to examine adsorption energies, activation barriers, gel polymerization, ion exchange, and heavy-metal binding, connecting interfacial mechanisms to macroscopic strength, durability, and contaminant retention.

Related manuscript: Zhan & Zheng (2026), *Composition-topology coupling programs clay-gel interphase assembly in alkali-activated aluminosilicates* (**in preparation**).

## Contaminant sequestration

We investigate how nanoplastics and other contaminants interact with clay surfaces, how chemical composition affects contaminant adsorption and remobilization, and how these molecular interactions influence clay aggregation, pore structure, transport, and sealing performance.

Related manuscript: Dai & Zheng (2026), *Nanoplastics as Sinks or Mobilizers: PFAS Partitioning, Mobility, and Retention in Clay–Water Systems* (**in preparation**).

## PFAS in organic-rich sediments

PFAS are partitioned among dissolved phases, suspended particulate matter, and clay-rich sediments. We investigate how clay minerals, natural organic matter chemistry and content, and salinity determine PFAS adsorption, transport, and retention.

Related manuscript: Lu & Zheng (2026), *Molecular controls on PFAS retention and remobilization in organic-rich sediments* (**in preparation**).

## Multiscale soft materials

We develop coupled multiphase-flow and deformation methods based on the Darcy–Brinkman–Biot (DBB) framework, including non-isothermal flow in deformable porous materials. Our OpenFOAM solver, [hybridBiotThermalInterFoam](https://github.com/xiaojinz/hybridBiotThermalInterFoam), models flow–deformation coupling across pore and Darcy scales.

Relevant publication: Zheng & Bourg (2025), *Water Resources Research*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025WR041300)

## Solute transport in soils

We study PFAS air–water interfacial retention and solute migration in variably saturated soils with coupled two-phase flow and deformation. Our volume-averaged, single-field framework has been verified against classical analytical transport solutions.

Related manuscript: Ning & Zheng (2026), *A single-field two-phase model for PFAS air-water interfacial retention in variably saturated soils* (**in preparation**).

## Radioactive waste disposal

We develop coupled thermal, hydraulic, chemical, radiological, and mechanical models for radionuclide transport and near-field stability, including decay chains, decay heat, sorption, gas generation, and rock deformation.

Related manuscripts (Ning & Zheng, 2026, **in preparation**): *Coupled thermo–hydro–chemical–radiological modelling of radionuclide fate and near-field stability around a buried heat-emitting waste source in marine clay*; and *An open-source, file-coupled two-way relay between a reactive-transport/decay-heat THC solver and elasto-plastic rock mechanics*.

## Reservoir simulation

Our CO₂ geological storage research includes compositional flow–geomechanics simulation of pressure monitoring above caprock, poroelastic pressure signals, and statistical models of heterogeneous fault sealing in sand–shale sequences.

Relevant publications:
- Zheng & Espinoza (2022), *Marine and Petroleum Geology*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.marpetgeo.2022.105961)
- Zheng et al. (2022), *International Journal of Greenhouse Gas Control*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijggc.2022.103660)

## Sedimentary rocks

We investigate fault-gouge transport properties, CO₂ breakthrough pressure and post-breakthrough permeability, and reservoir pressure management using fault-sealing experiments and uniaxial-strain compressibility measurements.

Relevant publications:
- Zheng & Espinoza (2021), *Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00603-021-02571-3)
- Zheng & Espinoza (2021), *Marine and Petroleum Geology*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.marpetgeo.2021.105054)

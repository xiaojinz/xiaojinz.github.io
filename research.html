RESEARCH

Connecting the microscopic to the engineering scale.

Our work spans fundamental mineral–fluid interactions to predictive simulations of geomaterials in environmental and energy applications.

01 / MATERIALS

Clay across scales

We examine clay–water interactions, swelling and hydration, cation exchange, clay aggregation, and the emergence of transport and mechanical properties across spatial scales. Atomistic and coarse-grained molecular dynamics connect nanoscale interactions with mesoscopic clay assemblies.

Key methods: all-atom MD, coarse-grained MD, statistical mechanics, multiscale upscaling.
02 / ENVIRONMENT

Engineered barriers and contaminant transport

We study bentonite barriers, waste containment systems, and the retention and mobility of PFAS, nanoplastics, radionuclides, and metals in clay-rich environments. Particular attention is given to mineral interfaces, natural organic matter, salinity, and evolving pore networks.

Applications: radioactive waste isolation, landfill liners, contaminated sediments, clay–geopolymer systems.
03 / COMPUTATION

Multiphase flow and coupled multiphysics

We develop numerical methods to simulate fluid flow, heat transfer, solute and reactive transport, and mechanical deformation in complex porous media. Our work builds on Darcy–Brinkman–Biot formulations and integrates processes that are often treated separately.

Key tools: OpenFOAM, coupled PDE solvers, continuum geomechanics, multiphase transport.
04 / GEO-ENERGY

Energy and subsurface infrastructure

We apply multiscale models to carbon geological storage, hydrogen generation and storage, geothermal applications, and the performance of engineered underground systems. Linking mineral-scale processes to field-scale behavior supports safer and more efficient subsurface technologies.

Applications: CO₂ storage, serpentinization, underground heat storage, fractured rock systems.
